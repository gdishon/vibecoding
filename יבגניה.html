The Teenage Sleep Crisis

Getting enough sleep is essential for teenagers, yet many struggle to achieve a full night's rest. Research shows that during adolescence, there is a natural biological shift that makes teens want to stay up later. However, biology is not the only factor.
Modern technology plays a significant role in preventing students from sleeping. The blue light emitted by smartphones and tablets can severely disrupt the brain's natural sleep cycle by delaying the release of melatonin. Experts highly recommend keeping electronic devices out of the bedroom.
Furthermore, chronic lack of sleep can lead to serious consequences. It may significantly decrease cognitive functions, such as memory and concentration in school. Additionally, it affects emotional stability, making teenagers more prone to stress and anxiety.
In a recent 2023 survey conducted in Tel Aviv, 85% of high school teachers noted a dramatic decrease in student concentration. Dr. Smith, who led the study, emphasized that tired students struggle to process new information effectively.
To avoid these detrimental issues, students must establish a consistent bedtime routine. Going to bed at the same time every night and avoiding screens an hour before sleep can dramatically improve overall sleep quality and academic performance.
